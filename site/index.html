Who are you

Sixteen people walk in here. Five sit down tonight and one of the five is you. Every question you ask is a question about which of these you are.

Some pairs are separated by exactly one thing. Kestrel and Magpie differ by a hat. One lie about a hat turns you into somebody else entirely.

Chapter

The pause is evidence

It proves nothing. A slow answer can be an honest one, and a practised liar answers on the beat.

But every hesitation at that table is on the record, and everyone knows it — which makes the pause itself something you have to perform. That is the whole scene, really. Not the questions. The four seconds afterwards, while four people decide what your four seconds meant.

Chapter

Call him a liar

The barman puts five glasses in front of you the first time you sit down. Asking is free. Answering is free. Lying is free.

Shrike
Liar

A glass buys one thing: the right to stop the room.

You pay it to the person you are accusing, before anything is checked — you buy them a drink, then call them a liar to their face. Then the house opens the verdict. Caught them, and they are out and the glass stays yours. Wrong, and you are out, and they have already been paid for the insult.
